Build Your X Recruiting Profile

From Coach Cole Murphy, 3Point Kicking

College coaches check X, often before they ever watch your full Hudl. Make your profile do the work: in five seconds a coach should know who you are, what you kick, and how to reach you. Here's how I want my specialists set up.

1. Profile basics

  • Handle: use your real name, plus your position or class if you need it (e.g. @FirstLastK28). No jokes or nicknames.
  • Display name: First Last plus your position or class, e.g. "Jake Rivera K/P '28".
  • Photo: a clear headshot or game photo in your school uniform. Your face should be visible.
  • Header: an action shot (follow-through, punt, snap) or a clean graphic with your class, position, school and your NCAA ID (from the NCAA Eligibility Center). Coaches use your NCAA ID to find you and add you to their recruiting board, so make it easy to see.

2. Bio checklist

Fit it into the bio and website field:

  • Class (2027, 2028…)
  • High school and city/state
  • Position: K / P / LS
  • Height / weight
  • GPA (and test score if it's strong)
  • Hudl link (put it in the website field)
  • Trackman link or numbers
  • Email address coaches can use
  • "Trains @3PointKicking"

Tip: if you need more than one link, put a link page in the website field and list Hudl and Trackman in the bio.

3. Pinned post

Pin a short film clip (30-60 seconds) of your best reps, with Trackman numbers on screen (FG distance, operation or snap time, drift). Include class, position, height/weight, GPA and email in the text. Update it every season. If you commit, pin your commitment post instead.

4. What to post, and when

  • After every game: FG x/y (long), PATs, kickoff touchbacks, punt average or hang, snaps. Post the same night or the next morning, and add a photo or short clip to every post. Posts with a picture get seen a lot more.
  • Camp results: placement, distances, invites. Post them within a day.
  • Trackman sessions: a clip and the key numbers every few weeks in the offseason.
  • Offers and visits: a graphic and a thank-you to the staff.
  • Academic wins: GPA updates, honor roll, test scores. Coaches recruit students too.

Keep it consistent: one good post beats five random ones.

5. Who to tag

  • Your high school program.
  • @3PointKicking, so I can repost you.
  • Camps you competed at (tag the camp and its coaches).
  • A college's special teams coach or program, only when it's relevant: an offer, a visit, their camp, or a direct thank-you. Don't tag 20 coaches on every post.

6. Stay professional

  • No negativity, complaining, trash talk or politics.
  • Coaches see your likes, reposts and replies. Clean them up.
  • Keep the account public. A private account can't be recruited.
  • Before you post, ask yourself: would I be fine with a head coach reading this out loud?

7. Follow coaches (the right way)

  • Follow the programs you're interested in, plus their special teams coordinators and recruiting staff.
  • Follow camps and specialist recruiting services.
  • Don't spam. No mass follows, no unrequested DMs with film every week. When a coach follows you back, send one short, polite DM with your Hudl, Trackman and email.

3PK clients: bring your profile to your College Pathway meeting and I'll go through it with you: bio, pinned post, who to follow and what to post next. — Coach Cole

NCAA recruiting calendar 2026–27

You can email, call or DM a college coach any time. The calendar only limits when coaches can reach out to you or meet in person.

Contact Coaches can meet you and your family in person.Evaluation Coaches can watch you play or practice, but can’t talk with you off campus.Quiet In-person contact only on that college’s campus.Dead No in-person contact. Calls, texts and emails are still allowed.
